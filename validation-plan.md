# Validation Plan

## Hypotheses
1. Candidates want a structured place to connect skills with evidence.
2. Reviewers can find relevant work faster in an evidence-linked profile than in a plain list.
3. Users understand that adding evidence does not mean the skill or credential is independently verified.

## Participants
- 5 students or early-career candidates actively preparing a résumé
- 5 people who have reviewed portfolios, interns, or entry-level applicants

## Candidate tasks
1. Add “Python” and attach a GitHub/project link.
2. Find a card that still needs evidence.
3. Edit a skill note.
4. Preview the profile and explain what a reviewer would see.

## Reviewer tasks
1. Find a project demonstrating Python.
2. Identify what evidence is user-submitted.
3. Explain what additional information would be needed before trusting a skill claim.

## Measures
- Task completion rate
- Time on task
- Number of assistance requests
- Misinterpretation rate for verification labels
- Post-task confidence (1–5)
- Qualitative feedback

## Decision rule
Continue to a higher-fidelity build only if at least 4/5 candidate participants complete the core task without help, at least 4/5 reviewers find evidence quickly, and no more than 1/5 participants mistake a submitted link for an independently verified credential. These are directional thresholds for a small qualitative study, not statistically representative results.

## Interview questions
- How do you currently organize evidence for each skill?
- Tell me about the last time you needed to prove a skill in an application.
- What makes a project link credible or useful?
- What would make you distrust a skills profile?
- Would you use this again for another application? Why or why not?
